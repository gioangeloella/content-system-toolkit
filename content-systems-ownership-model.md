# Content systems ownership model

A framework for defining what a content systems function owns, partners on, and does not do.

## Purpose

When content design shifts from embedded execution to a systems-level function, the most common mode of failure is ambiguity. Without clear boundaries, the role can become whatever the loudest stakeholder wants it to be in any given week. This model creates that clarity.

## The three-pillar model

If your content systems function sits within a design operations team, it likely shares space with other pillars. Here's one way to structure it:

| Pillar | Focus | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| Design Ops | How design happens — process, workflows, artifacts, rituals | Design leadership, designers, tool partners |
| Design System | Components, tokens, patterns, and accessibility guidance | PMs, engineers, product designers |
| Content Systems | Voice model, decision trees, and AI-assisted content infrastructure | Design, engineering, and PM as independent content owners |

All pillars share unified leadership and strategy so that process, patterns, and content architecture work in lockstep.

Adapt this model to your org. What's important is that content systems is a distinct function with a defined audience.

## Ownership boundaries

This is the most important section. Fill it in before socializing the role with leadership.

### We own

- Voice models, UX writing principles, content standards, and patterns
- AI content tooling, prompt systems, and decision frameworks
- Escalation paths and quality governance
- Content system lifecycle: versioning, monitoring, and iteration

### We partner on

- Design system component documentation and content naming conventions
- String repositories and content integration into design tool libraries
- Design ops onboarding and enablement materials
- Cross-functional content training and change management

### We do not

- Write copy for individual flows or features
- Review every screen before it goes to production
- Act as an embedded content resource for product teams
- Own content quality for feature launches, which are owned by product teams

The "we do not" section does more heavy lifting than any other. Writing down what you will not do is how a systems role protects itself from becoming the old role with a new title.

## Content support model

Once you've defined ownership, designers need to know how to get help. Here's how you can define your channels:

| Channel | Purpose | Response time |
|---|---|---|
| AI writing tool (e.g., Gem, Claude Project) | First stop for most content decisions | Instant (self-serve) |
| Slack channel | Quick questions and async input | 1 business day SLA |
| Office hours | Flow reviews, tool demos, and content decisions that need additional thought partnership | Scheduled weekly |

## How to customize this template

1. Adjust the three-pillar model to reflect your org structure. Bear in mind not every team has a design system or design ops function.
2. Rewrite the "we own / we partner on / we do not" lists to reflect your actual scope. Be specific. Vague boundaries will invite scope creep.
3. Define your support channels and response times based on your capacity.
4. Socialize the model with design leadership before sharing it broadly. Get buy-in on the "we do not" section first — that's where disagreements can surface.
